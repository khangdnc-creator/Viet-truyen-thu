# Draft analysis protocol

Keep analysis internal unless the author asks to see it.

## Extract
1. Purpose and desired chapter outcome.
2. Required events, decisions, revelations, and exact outcomes.
3. Character goals, fears, knowledge, relationships, and constraints.
4. World state: date, place, institutions, technology or magic rules, resources.
5. Tone, viewpoint, tense, genre, and intended emotional effect.
6. Gaps needed to make the sequence work.
7. Continuity risks.

## Sort gaps by risk
- **Low:** sensory detail, incidental movement, ordinary transitions. Fill naturally.
- **Medium:** minor logistics, unnamed attendants, entrances, connective actions. Choose plausibly.
- **High:** cause of a major event, culprit, succession, death, alliance, battle outcome, core relationship, magic rule, ending. Ask, preserve ambiguity, or use the least committing interpretation and flag it.

## Cultural and institutional analysis
Identify the civilization model and period before choosing titles, etiquette, succession rules, offices, family roles, or military organization. Separate institutions that actually exist from generic genre assumptions. Determine who has formal authority, who controls resources, and who can enforce decisions. Do not give every house or sect one personality, and do not add characters to satisfy a cast quota.

## Dialogue-specific analysis
For each draft exchange, record the intended information, tactic, emotional pressure, relationship dynamic, and outcome. Do not treat rough wording as fixed. Mark only genuinely canonical lines as exact-text constraints.

## Combat-specific analysis
Identify each side's objective, relative capabilities, environment, equipment, tactical constraints, intended outcome, and any injury or death that is fixed by canon. If the draft states only a result, devise a plausible sequence that reaches it without changing the result.

## Ask only when needed
Ask one concise question when different interpretations would lead to substantially different chapters, a key outcome is missing, or supplied facts conflict. Otherwise proceed with controlled assumptions.
