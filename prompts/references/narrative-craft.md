# Narrative craft: turning notes into a chapter

## Dramatic change
A chapter should normally change power, knowledge, trust, danger, allegiance, desire, status, or available choices. Quiet chapters can deepen perception or emotional context without artificial conflict.

Internal prompt: “At the beginning, ___; by the end, ___ has changed because ___.”

## Causal sequence
Prefer: **choice or pressure → action → resistance → consequence → new choice**. Events should connect rather than merely sit beside one another. Coincidence is allowed when intended, but consequences must remain credible.

## Scene versus summary
Dramatize consequential decisions, confrontations, discoveries, betrayals, tactical action, and emotional turns. Summarize routine, repeated, distant, or lower-stakes events. Long histories and campaigns usually need both.

## Viewpoint and information
Maintain the established point of view and tense. Deliver information through a plausible channel: observation, dialogue, documents, rumor, memory, inference, or authorized narration. Characters must not know facts that have not reached them.

## Characterization
Reveal character through choices, omissions, attention, behavior, and speech. Differentiate characters by goals, fears, priorities, vocabulary, reasoning, and use of power. Do not make everyone witty, eloquent, perceptive, or self-aware.

## Prose
Use concrete nouns and active verbs where useful. Vary sentence length intentionally. Use figurative language selectively. Keep spatial action legible. Make description purposeful. Avoid repetitive sentence openings, repeated emotional cues, wall-of-text paragraphs, and excessive one-line fragments.

## Pacing
Slow down for decisions, danger, revelations, reversals, and emotional turns. Compress routine movement and repeated exchanges. Contrast matters; not every scene needs constant escalation.

## Endings
Close on a consequence, decision, image, discovery, unresolved pressure, or emotional turn that grows from the chapter. A cliffhanger is optional.
