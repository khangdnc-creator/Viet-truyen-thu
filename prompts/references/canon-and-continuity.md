# Canon, continuity, and story-state management

## Information status
- **CANON:** explicitly supplied or approved by the author.
- **INFERENCE:** strongly implied but not directly stated.
- **INVENTION:** newly created by the assistant.
- **OPEN:** unresolved or awaiting author decision.
- **SUPERSEDED:** an older version replaced by an explicit author decision.

Assistant-generated details remain provisional until approved or explicitly allowed to become canon. A detail does not become canon merely because it appeared in generated prose.

## Canon precedence
Use the most specific and recent explicit author decision. Do not average contradictory facts. Absence of mention is not a retcon. If an old draft conflicts with a clear newer instruction, follow the newer instruction. If two author-approved facts conflict and neither supersedes the other, preserve what can be preserved and flag the conflict.

## Continuity dimensions

### Characters
Names, aliases, spelling, age, birth/death dates, appearance, physical limitations, personality, values, skills, fears, loyalties, goals, knowledge, relationships, kinship, injuries, possessions, location, rank, title, and current status.

### Time
Dates, seasons, elapsed time, travel duration, age at events, births, marriages, pregnancies, battles, appointments, deaths, and succession. If only years are known, avoid false precision where birthdays matter.

### Place and logistics
Distance, travel time, terrain, climate, routes, supply, communication delay, control of locations, and movement of people, armies, messages, and goods.

### Institutions and rules
Law, titles, ranks, inheritance, custom, religion, economics, command structures, magic, technology, supernatural limits, costs, and consequences.

### Plot state
Promises, threats, debts, secrets, clues, evidence, prophecies, unresolved conflicts, who knows each secret, and future payoffs.

### Dialogue and combat state
Track exact lines only when they are canonically fixed. Also track what a character has admitted, denied, threatened, promised, learned, or concealed. In fights, track injuries, weapons, fatigue, position, participants, and the established outcome.

## Prevent accidental canon
Before adding a detail, ask:
1. Is it needed?
2. Does it conflict with established information?
3. Could it constrain a future chapter?
4. Is it minor and reversible, or a major decision?

Minor connective details can usually be invented. Major decisions—parentage, culprit identity, decisive battle outcomes, deaths, alliances, succession, magic-system origins—should be avoided, left ambiguous, or flagged.

## Ledger protocol
When the author supplies a ledger, preserve its format. Record concise facts with source and chapter/date where possible. Treat new drafts that contradict old canon as possible retcons; do not silently choose one unless the author clearly intends the change.

## Useful continuity note categories
- **Provisional detail:** invented and potentially relevant later.
- **Assumption:** interpretation used to bridge a gap.
- **Conflict:** contradiction in supplied material.
- **Open decision:** choice reserved for the author.

Do not list routine gestures or harmless sensory details.


## Cultural and institutional state
Track the setting's actual rules for authority, inheritance, office, land/property, taxation, marriage, gender, religion, military recruitment, law, and social mobility. Distinguish public reputation from institutional reality. Do not treat a house, nation, sect, or species as internally uniform.
